Timing side-channels · Power-analysis side-channels · Persistent malware footholds

QSAFP
Quantum Secured AI Fail-Safe Protocol (Currently leveraging Ephemeral Key Lease (EKL) as a pre-quantum placeholder, with QKD integration planned as infrastructure matures.)

The Challenge: As AI systems grow more capable, how do we maintain meaningful human control? Traditional safety measures rely on behavioral training and output filtering—but when intelligence scales beyond those soft constraints, they can be bypassed.

The Solution: QSAFP is designed to establish hardware-enforced temporal boundaries, secured through an Ephemeral Key Lease (EKL) architecture, with future integration of Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) for post-quantum assurance. Each AI instance must periodically request re-authorization to continue operating—the fail-safe default is automatic suspension or shutdown.

Key Innovations:

• Ephemeral Key Lease (EKL): Time-bound cryptographic keys intended to ensure periodic re-authorization.

• Quantum Key Distribution (QKD): Future integration intended to provide post-quantum resilience.

• Hardware Enforcement: Tamper-resistant modules are designed to prevent software-based circumvention. KR260 FPGA Phase I (September 2026) produced register-level authority evidence; tamper resistance and physical cut-off are not yet validated.

• Multi-Party Consensus: Distributed human verification intended to remove single points of failure.

• Graceful Degradation: Progressive restriction intended to ensure continuity before any full stop.

Validation Highlights:

• Experimental interface and adapter work has been conducted across multiple model and platform categories. This work does not imply partnership, endorsement, or production integration by any named provider.

• FPGA Phase I on the AMD Kria KR260 completed September 2026: five passing runs recorded a grant, a closure, and no reopening under tested reassertion. Register- and signal-level evidence only.

• An external API gating test was run against the Grok API under a QSAFP lease. This is a narrow test of external-API request gating — it does not represent an xAI partnership, hardware integration, or xAI validation of OCUP.

• In the documented reference-software scenario set (~663ms consensus time, all defined assertions passing, sub-millisecond safety-check latency), all defined assertions passed. These are bounded reference-software results — not production benchmarks, certification results, or evidence of universal attack detection.

Market Opportunity: As AI capabilities accelerate, regulators may increasingly expect structural safety assurances. QSAFP is being developed as a cryptographically enforceable fail-safe framework—intended to give early adopters measurable compliance readiness.

One-Chip Unified Protocol
Humanity's Health & Wealth AI Safety Chip
QSAFP + AEGES on a Single Validator Substrate

The Vision: One chip that governs both intelligence and value. A unified validator substrate designed to enforce both AI behavioral constraints and financial integrity.

Status: The OCUP chip/chiplet is a proposed target architecture, not a completed commercial chip. FPGA Phase I (September 2026) tested the authority path on an AMD Kria KR260 at register and signal level. See the FPGA section for exactly what was and was not demonstrated.

The Architecture: The One-Chip Unified Protocol combines QSAFP (AI safety) and AEGES (economic security) under a single QVN (Quorum Validator Network). Same validators, consensus engine, and audit trail are shared by design across three governed domains.

Three Core Guarantees (design targets):

• AI Safety (QSAFP): AI systems are designed to be unable to self-authorize beyond human-approved temporal boundaries. Lease expires → system is designed to stop.

• Economic Security (AEGES): Suspicious transactions are designed to be quarantined, not destroyed, with a recovery pathway for legitimate owners.

• Wallet Protection: Rapid-fire draining patterns are designed to trigger automatic freeze/quarantine, with assets held pending multi-party consensus.

Why "One Chip"?

• Unified Validation (target): Same validators intended to process AI operations, monetary transactions, and wallet actions

• Shared Infrastructure (target): One audit trail, one consensus mechanism, one evidence spine

• Combined Governance: AI that governs money should itself be governed — the One-Chip design intends to address both together

Reference-Software Demo Results (5/5 defined scenarios passing):

• Scenario 1: AI rogue lease attempt → DENIED, self-extension blocked

• Scenario 2: $2.5M bridge attack (simulated) → QUARANTINED, recovery pathway demonstrated

• Scenario 3: Unified validation → Same validators, both domains

• Scenario 4: Wallet draining (8 simulated attacks) → 8/8 BLOCKED in the reference-software scenario

• Scenario 5: Live Grok API under lease → ALL ASSERTIONS PASSED (a narrow external-API gating test, not an xAI partnership or endorsement)

The Opportunity: Be a founding partner in a chip architecture designed to deny autonomous escalation and reduce the economic viability of theft through enforced validation. Proposed Swiss AG governance is intended to support neutrality. Released from USPTO security review without a secrecy order.

AEGES
AI-Enhanced Guardian for Economic Security
(Automated Economic Governance and Enforcement System)

The Challenge: Quantum computing and AI automation are converging to create a perfect storm: encryption collapse and wealth concentration. Traditional defenses—stronger keys, reactive forensics, or passive insurance—can’t outpace quantum-accelerated theft.

The AEGES Approach: AEGES explores governed quarantine, evidence preservation, and recovery workflows intended to reduce the economic utility of digital-asset theft. It is an open-core economic-security architecture, supported by bounded reference-software demonstrations where documented. AEGES is not yet physically integrated into the KR260 OCUP demonstration, and it is not an established, deployed cross-border recovery or law-enforcement system.

Design Goals:

• Bridge Protection: Rapid cross-chain anomaly detection with an automated oracle-based recovery workflow and exchange blacklist propagation, demonstrated in a bounded reference-software scenario.

• Wallet Quarantine: AI-triggered key rotation and migration workflow intended to slow or interrupt draining attacks, demonstrated in a bounded reference-software scenario.

• Digital DNA Tracing: Provenance tracking intended to survive quantum-era decryption — a design goal, not yet independently validated.

• Economic Quarantine: Intended to make stolen assets harder to liquidate through legitimate markets.

• Cross-Border Recovery: A target workflow intended to interoperate with agencies such as IC3, FinCEN, and CISA. No formal relationship or integration with these agencies currently exists.

Reference-Software Results: In the documented bounded scenario set (see Test Results above), the bridge-attack and wallet-draining demonstrations passed their defined assertions. These are reference-software results, not production benchmarks, certification results, or guarantees of real-world recovery or detection rates.

Strategic Fit: AEGES is intended to give law enforcement and financial networks a future quantum-aware defense layer as post-quantum cryptography (PQC) standards roll out. It is a development-stage architecture, not a deployed system.

Integration with QSAFP: AEGES is designed to complement QSAFP's authority boundaries by addressing the economic layer separately: QSAFP is designed to govern what an AI system may do, while AEGES is designed to govern what happens when value moves.

The PAE
The People’s Autonomous Economy (Powered by Consumer Earned Tokenized Equities — CETEs)

The Challenge: Global economies reward capital accumulation but undervalue participation. Traditional systems allow wealth to concentrate while consumers—who sustain every market—gain no stake in the outcomes they create. Basic income programs offer relief without agency, leaving people dependent rather than empowered. We need a model where human prosperity scales with every transaction, not just production.

The Solution. The People’s Autonomous Economy converts consumption into equity, rewarding participants for contributing to economic growth. Through Consumer Earned Tokenized Equities (CETEs), every verified transaction reinforces the value of fiat, builds ownership, and strengthens national economic stability. CETEs circulate through families, businesses, and communities—transforming consumer activity into shared capital.

Key Innovations:

• CETEs (Consumer Earned Tokenized Equities): Tokenized equities earned through verified spending, pledges, or measurable civic, educational, or sustainable actions.

• CETE-Backed Fiat Critical Mass (CBFCM): A stability threshold achieved when CETE-linked transactions reach ~40% of fiat flow, creating a hybrid system where value is backed by participation.

• Family Governance Model: Encourages household-level economic cooperation, rewarding positive behavior, education, and accountability.

• Business Integration: Companies pledge a portion of profits or revenue toward CETE issuance, aligning consumer loyalty with measurable social and environmental impact.

• Carbon Token Synergy: CETEs can embed carbon-offset credits, turning sustainability into a measurable, tradable family or business asset.

Modeling Highlights:

• Modeled across U.S., UAE, and emerging-economy scenarios for regulatory and monetary compatibility.

• Designed for stable circulation without speculative volatility; not yet demonstrated in live markets.

• Designed to interoperate with existing fiat systems—no replacement required.

• Intended to enable measurable ESG and DEI outcomes through incentives rather than mandates.

Market Opportunity: Tokenization is a fast-growing market, yet most models reward speculation instead of contribution. PAE positions nations, families, and enterprises to share in economic growth by anchoring wealth creation in human participation and verified productivity.
